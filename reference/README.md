# Reference photos

Alex's own photos, one folder per location. Milestone M11 in `BRIEF.md` reworks each place to match its folder. Use them for shapes, colours, materials and layout. In the game, brand names stay as the plain lookalike wordmarks already used (see `BRIEF.md`).

Number plates and mailbox name labels are blurred on purpose; the repo is public.

## `lonyay-utca/` — Lónyay utca

**The Református gimnázium** (`street-view-1…`, `-2…`, `-3…`). The landmark of the street, and missing from the game today.

- A long red-brick block, about 4 storeys plus attic, running a big stretch of one side of the street.
- Cream plaster bands and window surrounds against the brick; rows of round-arched windows, paired, with small columns between them; an arcade of arches on the ground floor.
- A stepped gable with pinnacles near the middle, and a slim grey spire rising behind it.

**The other buildings.**

- 4–5 storey eclectic tenements in cream, sand, ochre and pale mint green; a few greyer and sootier.
- Ground floors are often rough rusticated sandstone blocks with tall barred windows and plain dark doors.
- Corner bay windows, small stone balconies with iron railings, heavy cornices.
- Next to the school: a tenement with brick-and-cream stripes and ornate window pediments; its ground floor has arched openings, one with a green door (a vet's surgery, "Állatorvos").
- A modern beige stone office block with a regular window grid at the SPAR corner, and other modern infill further down.

**The street.**

- One carriageway, cars parked on both sides, many at an angle or nose-in and half on the kerb. Parked cars are dense: almost no gaps.
- Granite kerbs; pavements of grey concrete slabs, patched in places.
- Wooden crate planters with small shrubs or young trees along the kerb line, every 15–20 m.
- Young street trees in some stretches; lamps on wall brackets; a few wires strung across the street.
- Road markings: a painted bike symbol in the lane, double yellow kerb lines, a white hatched no-parking box.
- Parking wardens in yellow hi-vis vests walking in pairs (a good NPC type: "parkolóőr").

**The SPAR corner** (`spar-corner.jpg`, `side-street-spar-pedestrian.jpg`).

- An ochre corner building with a rusticated ground floor and tall round-arched shop windows.
- Black fascia boards above the arches with the shop name; black lattice security grilles over the windows (pulled down when closed: use them for the 00:00–06:00 closed state).
- A retro red neon script sign, "Élelmiszer" (groceries), running along the facade around the corner, and a round green-and-red shop sign on a bracket at the corner.
- A red fire hydrant and short green bollards at the kerb.
- Around the corner: a pedestrian street paved in pinkish-grey square tiles, a row of green hoop bollards along the building, a long drainage channel, a trimmed hedge and a tree, and a modern building with steps down to a lower entrance.
- This is **not** the return point. The only SPAR return point stays the City Spar on Kinizsi utca. Use this corner as the style reference for every SPAR shopfront in the game, including the Kinizsi one, and add this corner as a decorative shopfront that cannot be entered.

**A gateway, inside** (`gateway-mailboxes-bins.jpg`). This is what every courtyard gateway (kapualj) should look like.

- Floor: black-and-white checkerboard terrazzo tiles with a border pattern.
- Walls: white tiles up to waist height, painted plaster above with simple pilasters.
- A wall of grey metal mailboxes with stickers and paper labels, and a small grey bin hanging on the wall.
- Dark grey-green double doors, half glazed with small panes, opening onto the bin area.
- Wheelie bins right behind the inner doors: dark blue with a yellow lid (recycling, where bottles are), dark blue, and green.

## `matyas-utca/` — Mátyás utca

A narrow side street, quieter than Lónyay: straight, one carriageway, cars parked bumper to bumper. The photos are mostly about the buildings at eye level, which is what the player sees most.

**The street** (`street-view-corner-washpoint.jpg`, `street-view-pavement.jpg`).

- Cars parked parallel along both kerbs with almost no gaps; one lane left free in the middle.
- A double yellow line painted on the kerb edge, a strip of granite setts between kerb and pavement, grey concrete pavement with patches and manhole covers.
- Wires and festoon lights strung across the street between the buildings; lamps on wall brackets.
- Official street-name plates on the corner buildings: white enamel with black serif text, e.g. "IX. ker. Ferencváros / Mátyás utca / 12 → 22". These are very recognisable; put them on every street corner, with the real street name and house-number range.
- Small enamel house-number plates by every gate, and little white utility signs (gas, water valve) on the walls.

**Buildings.**

- Left of the corner photo: a long ochre-mustard block, 4 storeys plus a row of dormers in the roof. Arched upper windows with heavy ornamental stone surrounds; a ground floor in horizontal bands of rustication with paired arched windows; a deep arched gateway with a wrought-iron balcony above it.
- The corner building on the right: grey-green render, a rounded corner tower, rusticated ground floor and a large arched shop window. Its shop is a self-service laundry with tall vertical white letters down the corner; in the game call it "MOSODA" (laundry) rather than the real brand.
- Further along: grey-beige tenements with roller shutters and rich window pediments; a pale blue-grey and yellow public building with the Hungarian and EU flags out front (a school or office); enclosed wooden bay loggias high up on one beige house; red tiled roofs and small turrets at the far end.
- `facade-no18-bay-windows.jpg`, No. 18: an apricot 4-storey eclectic tenement with two closed bay windows (zárt erkély) projecting on either side, white stucco window frames with shell and cartouche ornaments, an egg-and-dart cornice, half-lowered roller shutters. The ground floor is banded rustication; the gate sits in a stone frame with console brackets and two urn finials on top, under a glazed transom.
- `green-house-door.jpg`: a plain interwar house painted dark olive green on a grey stone plinth, with a concrete bay balcony overhanging the pavement, a boarded shop window and black flag-holder brackets.

**Gates and doors.** These photos show that no two gates look the same. Make gates a small kit of variants and mix them:

- **Carved brown double gate** (`carved-gate-door.jpg`): dark brown wood in an arched opening with a semicircular fanlight above, a dentil cornice, round wreath medallions with monograms, lion-head medallions low down, two small arched windows with wrought-iron scroll grilles, a white door knob, scuffs and a graffiti tag.
- **Brown double gate with oval windows** (No. 18): simpler panels, two oval glass windows with iron grilles, a stone surround.
- **Green panelled gate** (green house): tall dark green wooden doors with stepped geometric panels and a glazed transom, a modern intercom keypad with name plates beside it, a small security light above.
- Many gates have a small hanging mailbox or a paper notice taped on.

**Wear and tear.** This is what makes it feel real:

- Graffiti tags on almost every ground floor and plinth, rarely higher than 2.5 m. A set of 10–15 generic tag decals (invented, not copied) on random ground-floor walls would do a lot.
- Ground-floor plinths often painted a different colour (dark red, grey) from the facade above.
- Pavement under repair in places: patched asphalt, plywood boards over holes, a road-works post with yellow-and-black tape. A couple of these per run would be a nice obstacle.

## `raday-utca/` — Ráday utca

The café and bar street, and the busiest place in the game at night. It is paved, not asphalt: this fixes the "Ráday looks all-asphalt" gap in `CLAUDE.md`.

**Ground and street layout.**

- The whole street is block paving at one level, no tall kerbs. Pavements are light beige square tiles laid on the diagonal; the strip where cars drive is grey concrete paving blocks; a steel drainage channel runs between them.
- Car-free stretches (check OSM `highway=pedestrian`) and stretches where cars may drive one way, slowly, with a few parking bays (`lamp-flower-basket-bollards.jpg`, `waffle-terrace-hexagon-planters.jpg`).
- A long row of dark green cast-iron bollards with rounded tops separates the walking strip from cars, about every 1.5 m.
- Large square cast-iron utility covers in the pavement, round manhole covers in the middle.

**Street furniture.** This is the signature of Ráday, and different from the curly lamps everywhere else.

- **Lamps:** dark green cast-iron posts with a fluted base, a curly scroll arm at the top, and a copper bell-shaped shade over a glass globe. About every 20 m.
- **Hanging flower baskets** on the lamp posts at about 3.5 m: big round baskets of dark purple and lime-green trailing leaves with red flowers.
- Bike-share docks with lime-green bikes; small direction signs for the bike scheme on the lamp posts (use a generic "BUBI"-style green sign without the real logos).
- Blue parking and one-way signs, a yellow sticker-covered traffic beacon on a concrete base, string lights across the street.

**Terraces.** Most of the street width is restaurant terraces. Replace the current red-awning look with this mix:

- Wooden deck platforms raised a step above the paving, under awnings on black steel frames: dark brown or black awnings, cream ones, a long white one with festoon bulbs hanging from its edge.
- Furniture: folding beer tables and benches (light wood with green steel legs), café tables with wicker chairs, tall black planter pots, oleanders and small trees in pots, low green railings around each terrace, artificial grass mats.
- One terrace divider is a wall of colourful hexagon boxes (red, yellow, blue, pink, light grey) with plants in some of them. Great as a one-off landmark.
- **Time of day matters:** in the morning terraces are closed, with tables stacked or covered with dark green tarps and benches folded on the tables (`art-nouveau-house-beer-tables.jpg`, `pedestrian-part-terraces.jpg`). From late afternoon they open with people, and at night they are full. That is when party leftovers and bin bottles here should peak.
- Gilded picture-frame menu boxes on the facades, A-frame chalkboards on the pavement, Hungarian flags on brackets above doorways.

**Buildings.**

- A mix of cream, white and pale grey eclectic tenements with balconies and rounded corner bays, a grey zinc mansard roof, and one modern 1960s block with AC units on its balconies.
- **The Art Nouveau house** (`art-nouveau-house-beer-tables.jpg`): white secession facade with wavy bays and curving balconies, blue window frames, thin peach-and-blue tile stripes, green-and-orange ceramic fruit ornaments near the top, and a horseshoe-arched window on the ground floor. Worth hand-building as a landmark.
- Next to it, an ornate red-brick-and-cream neo-Renaissance tenement with deep dark eaves; another with yellow stucco window frames on brick (`waffle-terrace-hexagon-planters.jpg`).
- A building wrapped in scaffolding with green netting.
- **Ground-floor shops** (`ground-floor-shops.jpg`): every arch on the ground floor is a small business: a 24/7 vending-machine shop with a bright green sign, a Korean street-food place with flags, menus and delivery stickers, a coffee menu board, a wine shop. Steps up to the doors, AC units above. Use invented names in the game, e.g. "AUTOMATA 0–24", "KOREAI BÜFÉ", "BOROK".

**The empty lot** (`firewall-empty-lot.jpg`). A gap where a building is missing, opening onto a small paved square.

- Exposed firewalls (tűzfal) of the neighbours: bare old brick, stepped gables with chimneys, narrow light shafts with tiny windows, patched in many places.
- The lot is fenced off with green posts and red-white and yellow-black tape, with a gravel heap and loose paving stones inside.
- A graffiti-and-poster-covered electrical substation box, a mural on a wall, big dark green planter boxes, bike-share bikes.
- Gameplay idea: make the fenced lot a hidden spot that is always worth checking, behind the substation box.

## `erkel-torok-pal-utca/` — Erkel utca and Török Pál utca

Two side streets photographed together. Alex hasn't said which photo is which street yet; match them with OSM (the old low house is a single-storey building, and in `street-toward-market-hall.jpg` the far end of the street looks straight at the market hall). Apply the general findings to both streets.

**A new public bin design** (`old-low-house-graffiti.jpg`). This is what a Budapest public bin looks like, and the game's public bins should copy it: a dark green perforated steel basket hanging in a green steel hoop frame fixed to the ground, with a mint-green bin bag folded over the rim. Use it for every public bin.

**Lamps hung over the street.** In these side streets there are no lamp posts: round dish lamps hang on wires stretched across the street between the facades, at about 7–8 m. Use this instead of posts in narrow side streets (roughly under 12 m between facades), and keep posts for the main streets and Ráday.

**The street.**

- Patched asphalt road with yellow cycle-lane markings: a bike symbol with double chevrons painted on the road in each direction (shared lane).
- A white hatched parking box, a blue "P" sign, a no-stopping sign with a tow-away plate under it, yellow paint on the kerb where you can't stop.
- Kerbs edged with granite setts; pavements of plain asphalt or grey slabs, with beige tiles at the corners.
- Rows of black bollards with ball tops along the kerb.
- Parked cars plus delivery vans and a small flatbed truck; a block of construction fence on the road.

**Buildings.**

- `grey-palace-street.jpg`: a long, grand grey-beige neo-Renaissance block, 4 storeys. Strongly rusticated ground floor with tall round-arched windows under heavy stone arches, a dark red service door, a granite plinth with graffiti. Above: windows with triangular and segmental pediments on consoles, a deep dentil cornice. Opposite, a whole building under scaffolding wrapped in green netting.
- `street-toward-market-hall.jpg`: an older, plainer pale yellow neoclassical block, 3–4 storeys, with arched niches over the ground-floor windows; a red-orange facade further down. Left: a blue-grey painted shopfront with dark wooden windows and a "KIADÓ" (for rent) banner. **Keep this sightline:** the yellow-brick gable of the market hall, with trees in front, closes the view at the end of the street.
- `old-low-house-graffiti.jpg`: a rare survivor, an old single-storey house with peeling yellow plaster, a steep brown tiled roof, a brick chimney, small windows and arched doors, covered in big colourful graffiti pieces from the pavement up to the eaves. A small restaurant terrace in front: tables with red-and-white checked cloths, black bistro chairs, green artificial grass, a closed parasol, wall lanterns. Behind it a tall bare firewall rises. Make this house a landmark; it reads instantly as "not a tenement".
- Opposite it: a long ochre building with arched ground-floor openings and an octagonal restaurant sign (invent the name).
